## The 9:16 Frame: A Different Canvas

Shooting for vertical isn't just rotating your camera. The composition rules are fundamentally different.

### Subject Placement

In 16:9 horizontal video, the rule of thirds places your subject slightly off-center on a wide canvas. In 9:16, the frame is tall and narrow. Optimal placement changes:

- **Face content:** Eyes at the upper third of the frame
- **Action content:** Subject centered with vertical headroom above
- **Text-heavy content:** Clear vertical zones for text overlays

### The Safe Zone

Platform UI elements—likes, comments, shares, profile icons—occupy different areas on different apps. There's a universal "safe zone" for your primary subject: roughly centered horizontally, between 15% from the top and 30% from the bottom.

Anything outside this zone risks being obscured by UI.

### Motion Direction

Vertical framing changes how motion reads:

- **Upward movement** feels aspirational and energetic
- **Downward movement** feels weighty or ominous  
- **Lateral movement** draws the eye but can exit the frame quickly

Plan your shot's action vector before you hit record.

## Reframing Horizontal to Vertical: The AI Approach

If your primary content is horizontal—streams, podcasts, webinars—you need smart reframing, not just a crop.

<Tabs items={["The Problem", "Smart Reframe", "Best Practices"]}>
  <Tab>
    ### The Old Way: Dumb Cropping

    A simple center crop of a 16:9 video cuts off anything that isn't dead-center. Two-person conversations lose one face. B-roll footage loses context. The result looks like someone cut holes in your video.

    Most creators have seen the results: awkward framing, missing context, subjects cropped at the neck.
  </Tab>
  <Tab>
    ### AI-Powered Smart Reframing

    Modern AI reframing systems use:

    **Face tracking** — Identifies the primary speaker and keeps their face centered, even as they move or gesticulate.

    **Multi-subject handling** — When two speakers are on screen, it alternates focus based on who's talking (detected via audio).

    **Semantic content awareness** — Recognizes when text, graphics, or key visual elements should remain in frame.

    **Motion prediction** — Anticipates where the subject is moving and adjusts the crop window proactively, not reactively.
  </Tab>
  <Tab>
    ### Production Best Practices for Reframing

    Even with AI, a few production choices make reframing easier:

    - **Shoot with reframe headroom in mind:** Position subjects in the center of horizontal frame, not the extremes
    - **Use a plain background:** Complex backgrounds look cluttered at 9:16 proportions
    - **Single-speaker fronted:** For podcasts, consider a solo talking-head shot alongside the wide shot specifically for reframing
    - **Avoid side-by-side text:** Horizontal text overlays often get cut off in vertical reframes
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Building a Vertical-First Production Setup

If you're ready to shoot native vertical, here's what you need:

<Steps>
  <Step>**Phone or camera with vertical mounting** — Most modern mirrorless cameras and all smartphones support 9:16 natively. Use a vertical phone mount or rotate your camera rig.</Step>
  <Step>**Background optimization** — Vertical framing shows more of your ceiling and floor. A clean, intentional backdrop becomes more important, not less.</Step>
  <Step>**Lighting for vertical** — Light from above or slightly front-above works for face content. Avoid side lighting that casts harsh shadows across the narrow frame.</Step>
  <Step>**Microphone placement** — With a taller frame, boom mics become more visible. Lavalier (lapel) mics are often preferable for native vertical production.</Step>
  <Step>**Caption zone planning** — Decide whether captions go in the bottom third (standard) or overlay the center. Plan your shot to leave room.</Step>
</Steps>

The shift to vertical-first isn't just technical—it's mental. It means:

- Planning your best moments to be captured in portrait before you record
- Thinking about how your content story works within a tall, narrow frame
- Designing your brand identity to work vertically as well as horizontally

The creators who've made this shift don't think about "converting" their content to vertical anymore. They think vertical from the start—and the results show it.
