Welcome to ClypAI. This guide will walk you through your first session—from upload to export—so you can see exactly what the platform does and how to get the most from it.

You'll have your first clip ready in about 10 minutes.

## Before You Start

You'll need:
- A video file (MP4, MOV, or MKV — up to 4GB for free accounts)
- A ClypAI account ([sign up here](https://clyp.ai))
- 10 minutes

<Callout type="info" title="Best Content for Your First Session">
  Start with content that's at least 15 minutes long. Short content doesn't give the AI enough material to identify strong moments. Ideal first files: a podcast episode, a stream VOD, a recorded webinar or presentation, or a recorded interview.
</Callout>

## Step 1: Upload Your Video

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **From the dashboard**, click the **New Project** button in the top-right corner.

    You'll be prompted to name your project. Use something descriptive—"Podcast Ep. 47" is more useful than "Recording 1."
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Upload your file** by dragging it into the upload zone or clicking to browse. ClypAI supports:
    - **MP4** (most common, recommended)
    - **MOV** (Apple devices, Final Cut exports)
    - **MKV** (common for OBS recordings)
    - **WebM** (browser-based recordings)

    Large files (1–4GB) typically take 2–5 minutes to upload depending on your connection speed.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Set your content category.** Tell ClypAI what type of content this is:
    - Podcast / Interview
    - Live Stream
    - Educational / Tutorial
    - Presentation / Webinar
    - Commentary / Reaction

    This helps the AI model apply the right detection parameters. A comedy stream and a business webinar have different patterns for "a good moment."
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 2: AI Processing

Once your file is uploaded, ClypAI begins processing automatically. Here's what's happening:

<Tabs items={["Transcription", "Moment Detection", "Reframing Analysis"]}>
  <Tab>
    ### Audio Transcription

    ClypAI generates a full transcript of your video using a high-accuracy speech model. This typically completes in 20–40% of the video's runtime (a 60-minute video takes 12–24 minutes to transcribe).

    The transcript is used for:
    - Caption generation
    - Searchable content index
    - Text-based moment identification (key phrases, topic transitions)
  </Tab>
  <Tab>
    ### Moment Detection

    Simultaneously, the AI model analyzes both audio and video tracks to identify moments with high engagement potential. It's scoring:
    - Emotional intensity (voice energy, facial expressions)
    - Narrative completeness (does it stand alone?)
    - Hook quality (does it have a strong opening?)
    - Value delivery (is something genuinely communicated?)

    You'll see a score (0–100) for each identified moment.
  </Tab>
  <Tab>
    ### Smart Reframe Analysis

    The system identifies the primary subject in your video and pre-calculates optimal 9:16 reframe windows for each suggested clip. It tracks:
    - Face position over time
    - Active speaker (in multi-person content)
    - Important visual elements that should stay in frame

    This means your vertical clips are already optimally framed before you even open the editor.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Step 3: Review Your Clips

When processing is complete, you'll see a list of suggested clips sorted by AI score. Here's how to review them efficiently:

**The clip list shows:**
- Clip title (AI-generated from the content)
- Duration
- AI score (0–100)
- Timestamp in source video
- Preview thumbnail

**Keyboard shortcuts for fast review:**
- `Space` — play/pause current clip
- `→` — next clip
- `←` — previous clip
- `A` — approve clip
- `R` — reject clip
- `E` — open full editor

Most users find that clips scoring above 70 are almost always worth keeping. Clips between 50–70 are worth a quick watch. Below 50, your judgment call.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't Over-Edit on First Review">
  Your first pass through the clips should be a keep/skip decision only. Save the fine-tuning (exact cut points, hook adjustments) for the editing phase. Moving quickly through the initial review preserves your creative energy for the work that matters.
</Callout>

## Step 4: Edit Your Best Clip

Select your highest-scored clip and open the editor. The editing interface has three panels:

**Left panel: Timeline**
- Drag the in/out points to trim the clip
- Fine-tune to the exact frame
- Preview the trimmed version before committing

**Center panel: Preview**
- 9:16 preview with smart reframe applied
- Toggle between 16:9 (original) and 9:16 (vertical)
- Caption overlay preview

**Right panel: Captions**
- Full auto-generated caption track
- Click any word to correct it
- Drag to reposition caption block
- Style controls (font, size, color, animation)

For your first clip, spend no more than 5 minutes here. Get comfortable with the interface before optimizing.

## Step 5: Export

When you're ready to export:

<Steps>
  <Step>Click the **Export** button in the top-right of the editor.</Step>
  <Step>Select your target platform from the preset list (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, LinkedIn, or Custom).</Step>
  <Step>Review the export settings — the preset will have already configured the optimal resolution, frame rate, and caption format for your chosen platform.</Step>
  <Step>Click **Export Now** and wait for the download link.</Step>
</Steps>

Export typically takes 5–15 seconds for a 30–60 second clip.

## What to Do With Your First Clip

You have a clip. Now what?

**Post it.** Don't overthink it. Your first clip isn't going to be your best clip—it's going to be the first of many. The creators who grow are the ones who iterate quickly, not the ones who refine endlessly.

Watch the metrics after 24 hours. Look at:
- **3-second view rate** — what percentage of viewers watch past the first 3 seconds?
- **Completion rate** — what percentage watch to the end?
- **Engagement rate** — comments, shares, likes as a percentage of views?

These three metrics, tracked over time, tell you everything you need to know about whether your clips are improving.

## Getting the Most From ClypAI

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="Process sessions immediately, not later">
    Upload your recordings immediately after you finish them. The motivation to review clips fades fast. If you let recordings sit for days, they often sit for weeks. Build the habit: record, then upload.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Set your caption brand kit first">
    Before you export more than a few clips, take 10 minutes to configure your Caption Brand Kit (Settings → Brand Kit). Once it's set, every clip you export automatically uses your styling. This saves significant time and makes your content look immediately professional.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Don't ignore lower-scored clips">
    The AI score is a starting point, not a verdict. Some of the most resonant clips are quiet, subtle moments that don't score high on energy metrics. Browse through clips in the 40–60 range occasionally—you might find something the AI undervalued.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use the archive importer for old content">
    If you have a YouTube channel, Twitch account, or podcast with existing episodes, connect them via Settings → Integrations → Archive Import. ClypAI will scan your back catalog and identify high-value clips from content you published months or years ago.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
