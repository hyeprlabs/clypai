## The Case for Captions Is Settled. The Method Is Not.

85% of social media videos are watched without sound. Caption your videos or lose most of your audience — that part is no longer up for debate.

But *how* you caption your videos is a different conversation. Manual captions have long been considered the "gold standard." In 2026, that assumption deserves scrutiny.

## The Real Cost of Manual Captioning

<Callout type="warn">
  A professional captioner can produce roughly 5 minutes of captions per hour of work at high accuracy. For a 60-minute podcast, that's 12 hours of labor — or a professional services invoice north of $150.
</Callout>

Most creators don't have $150 or 12 hours per episode. They skip captions entirely, or produce a rushed, low-quality version that hurts more than it helps.

## What Modern AI Captioning Actually Looks Like

Here's where the conversation gets interesting. AI captioning in 2026 isn't the "auto-generated" disaster of 2018. Modern ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition) models achieve **94–97% word-error-rate accuracy** on clean podcast audio.

<Callout type="info">
  **Word Error Rate Explained**

  A 96% accuracy rate on a 1,000-word segment means approximately 40 errors. In practice, most are minor substitutions ("their" vs "there") that viewers read past without noticing. For social media captions, this is professional quality.
</Callout>

## When to Still Use Manual Captioning

<Callout type="warn">
  **Situations Where Manual Remains the Right Choice**

  Legal or compliance content (court transcripts, medical recordings), accessibility-critical content required to meet WCAG standards, or heavily accented speech in specialized professional domains — these deserve the extra precision of professional human captioners.
</Callout>

For the vast majority of content creators producing social media content? AI is not just "good enough" — it's faster, cheaper, and increasingly styled better than most manual alternatives.
