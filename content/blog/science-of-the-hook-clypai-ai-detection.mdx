## What Is a "Hook" and Why Does It Matter?

A hook is any moment in your content that would make a new viewer stop scrolling, watch to the end, and want more. It's the 3-second window that determines whether your clip gets watched or skipped.

Hooks aren't just a TikTok phenomenon. Every high-performing short-form clip — regardless of platform — has a strong hook at the start. The platforms' algorithms are literally optimized to distribute content with good hooks more widely.

<Callout type="info">
  Platform data consistently shows that videos with top-quartile hook completion rates (viewers who watch past the first 3 seconds) receive 4–8× more algorithmic distribution than videos with average hooks. The hook is the single highest-leverage element in short-form content.
</Callout>

## The 6 Signals ClypAI Analyzes

ClypAI's AI doesn't guess which moments are compelling. It analyzes six distinct signal categories to score every potential clip candidate:

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Linguistic Surprise Score

    Moments where the speaker says something unexpected, counterintuitive, or provocative score highly. The model is trained to recognize:

    - Contrarian statements ("Most people think X, but actually...")
    - Revelation structures ("And then we realized...")
    - Bold claims ("This is the biggest mistake creators make")
    - Numerical specificity ("94% of creators who do this fail within 6 months")

    These linguistic patterns reliably drive curiosity and watch-through.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Emotional Energy Detection

    Voice analysis measures pitch variance, speaking pace, and volume to identify emotional peaks. A speaker who suddenly speeds up and increases pitch is usually saying something they care about — and that energy is contagious on screen.

    The model distinguishes between:
    - Natural energy peaks (genuine enthusiasm, laughter, emphasis)
    - Monotone sections (explanatory content, recaps, transitions)
    - Emotional valleys (pauses, hesitations, filler sections)
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Narrative Completeness Score

    A clip that starts mid-thought and ends mid-sentence is confusing, regardless of how interesting the content is. ClypAI evaluates narrative completeness:

    - **Strong start signal:** The clip begins with a clear subject introduction or hook phrase
    - **Strong end signal:** The clip ends with a resolution, punchline, or clear conclusion
    - Clips with both high start and end signals are prioritized as fully-formed standalone pieces
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Semantic Importance Weighting

    Using a large language model, ClypAI evaluates the *meaning* of what's being said — not just how it's being said. Segments that contain:

    - Unique insights or data points
    - Actionable advice
    - Story resolution moments
    - Core thesis statements

    ...all receive higher semantic importance scores.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Visual Engagement Indicators

    For video content (not just audio), the visual layer adds additional signals:

    - Camera movement or cutting → signals intentional emphasis
    - Speaker's eye contact with camera → correlates with direct-address hooks
    - Gesture frequency → physical movement correlates with energy and emphasis
    - Screen content → if a speaker is pointing at a chart, they're probably making a key point
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Historical Performance Learning

    As you use ClypAI, the AI accumulates data on which of its suggestions you approve and reject. Over time, it learns your audience's specific preferences and your own editorial judgment.

    A creator whose audience loves data-heavy clips will see those ranked higher. A creator whose audience responds to vulnerability will see personal story moments prioritized. The model personalizes to *you*.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the AI Gets Wrong (and How We're Fixing It)

<Alert variant="warning">
  <AlertTitle>No AI Is Perfect at Hook Detection</AlertTitle>
  <AlertDescription>
    The model is excellent at pattern-matching for known hook structures. It struggles with highly niche or cultural content where the significance is contextually dependent — a moment that's hilarious to your specific community might appear neutral to the AI. Your feedback on rejected clips directly trains the model to improve on your content type.
  </AlertDescription>
</Alert>

The most common false positives:
- **Inside jokes** — High energy + surprise structure, but meaningless out of context
- **Technical jargon peaks** — Rapid energy spikes during acronym-heavy explanations score well on energy but poorly on general audience appeal
- **Cliffhangers mid-sentence** — High narrative setup score but poor completeness score; we're improving the resolution detection
